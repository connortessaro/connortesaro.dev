## A conversation is not yet a decision.

A team can produce a long thread without agreeing on what is actually in dispute. Different people hold different constraints, and the loudest or earliest answer can shape everything that follows.

I built Ringi to give those perspectives a structure. It works inside Slack: gather individual positions, identify the crux, write a brief, and put the recommendation back in front of the people affected.

## The system behind the conversation

Ringi separates the conversation from the decision state. A typed state machine governs the session, while gathering and synthesis handle the language. That separation matters because an eloquent model response should not be enough to move a session into the wrong state.

The implementation includes multi-workspace OAuth installation, stakeholder messaging, reminders, a Postgres database with Drizzle, and Langfuse instrumentation for model cost, latency, and quality. The project also includes model evaluations and fixtures for assessing synthesis.

### Gather before converging

Each stakeholder gets room to state a position independently. The point is to capture the constraints underneath an answer before asking everyone to respond to the same recommendation.

### Make the disagreement explicit

The resulting brief identifies a crux, presents options, and makes a recommendation. A useful brief should let someone recognize their concern and understand what the decision would change.

### Give objections a path

An objection does not disappear into another long thread. It opens a revision round so the recommendation can be reconsidered. The decision workflow records agreement explicitly.

## What I owned

I worked across the product design, conversation state machine, Slack integration, database, and deployment. The most interesting boundary was between flexible language and explicit application state: the conversation can vary, but transitions still need rules.

## A deliberate tradeoff

Ringi keeps the decision with the participants. That makes the interaction more involved than simply generating a summary, but the outcome carries the context of who agreed and what remained contested.

## Where it stands

Ringi is in early access. The public site includes a theater replay, and the implementation supports the end-to-end decision loop. I am not claiming measured adoption or time savings here; the next question is how well the workflow holds up for teams using it on their own decisions.
